# Contributing to the OpenSSF Global Cyber Policy Working Group

Welcome! We're excited that you're interested in contributing to the Global Cyber Policy Working Group. We aim to create a supportive, inclusive environment where everyone can thrive and make meaningful contributions.

## How to Connect with Us

- **Public Calendar:** Stay informed about our meetings, events, and activities by checking our [public calendar](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/u/0/r?cid=czYzdm9lZmhwNWk5cGZsdGI1cTY3bmdwZXNAZ3JvdXAuY2FsZW5kYXIuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbQ).

- **Slack Channel:** Join the conversation on our #wg-globalcyberpolicy [OpenSSF Slack channel](https://openssf.slack.com/archives/C084A6XPX0F). This is the best place to ask questions, share ideas, and collaborate.

## How to Contribute

We value all contributions, from improving documentation to participating in discussions and developing new initiatives. Here’s how you can get started:

### 1. Participate in Discussions

- Join our #wg-globalcyberpolicy channel via the [OpenSSF Slack channel](https://openssf.slack.com/archives/C084A6XPX0F).

- Attend working group meetings listed on the public calendar.

### 2. Report Issues or Suggest Enhancements

- Use the [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/ossf/wg-globalcyberpolicy/issues) tab to report problems or suggest ideas.
- Search existing issues, PRs, and [published resources](https://policy.openssf.org/) first.
- For a new deliverable or initiative, use the [roadmap proposal form](https://github.com/ossf/wg-globalcyberpolicy/issues/new?template=roadmap-proposal.yml).
  Ordinary fixes and questions can use a blank issue.

#### Roadmap proposals and non-code contributions

Read the [consolidated roadmap](ROADMAP.md) for proposed work and related records. A
proposal is not a WG-approved commitment, priority, delivery date, or assignment.
The Project is not yet launched; existing issue discussions remain the starting
point.

Describe the audience, problem, scope and non-goals, useful output, completion
criteria, dependencies, review needs, and next action. Reuse matching issues
without overwriting their authors' scope or removing existing assignees. A parent
initiative is only useful when it coordinates multiple deliverables.

Contributors can help by writing, reviewing policy interpretations, testing a
guide with its intended audience, providing examples, translating, or bringing
regional experience. Comment on an issue with the help you can offer; no coding
is required. Do not assign someone just because they joined a discussion.

Use issue comments for scope, coordination, and decisions; use PR review for
wording. Record useful meeting or Slack conclusions on the issue with permission.
Get publication clearance before copying private source material into a public
issue, PR, or branch, including a public fork.

Maintainers record acceptance and planning decisions under the existing
[charter](CHARTER.md) and [governance](governance/GOVERNANCE.md). Accepted work
needs a willing coordinator and a concrete next action before it is in progress.
Keep blockers explicit. Completion requires the agreed criteria and a link to
published material or delivered-activity evidence, not merely a merged draft.
Record a decision not to proceed separately from a delivered result.

### 3. Submit Pull Requests

- Fork the repository.
- Create a feature branch (`git checkout -b feature/your-feature-name`).
- Sign off commits as required by [governance](governance/GOVERNANCE.md)
  (`git commit -s -m 'Add your message here'`). Sign-off adds a `Signed-off-by`
  trailer; it is distinct from a cryptographic signature. See also the existing
  guidance on [signing commits](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/managing-commit-signature-verification/signing-commits).
- Push your branch (`git push origin feature/your-feature-name`).
- Open a Pull Request (PR) with a detailed description.
- Link the relevant issue. Do not use an automatic closing keyword for an entire
  initiative when the PR only delivers a draft or one part of it.

### 4. Review Process

- All PRs are reviewed by maintainers.
- [Governance](governance/GOVERNANCE.md) requires at least two maintainer approvals
  before merge. This rollout does not change the existing decision process.
- Ensure your PR is well-documented and follows project guidelines.
- Be open to feedback and ready to make improvements.

## Code of Conduct

We are committed to maintaining a welcoming, inclusive, and respectful environment. Please read and follow our [Code of Conduct](https://openssf.org/community/code-of-conduct/) to ensure a positive experience for everyone.

## Community Expectations

- **Be Respectful:** Value each other's ideas and contributions.
- **Be Collaborative:** Work together to solve problems and build new initiatives.
- **Be Open:** Share your knowledge and be open to learning from others.

We’re excited to collaborate with you and appreciate your support in advancing open source cybersecurity!

For any questions or additional guidance, contact us through Slack or during our working group meetings.
